The gap

Shopify secures the platform. It does not secure your configuration.

Every app, pixel, tag, and theme edit adds code that runs in your customer's browser. Most stores have no inventory of what runs at checkout, who owns it, or what it can read.

“We installed it for a Black Friday test and forgot it was still loading.” — the most common sentence in ecommerce security.

Fake checkout via a third-party app

Attackers have used compromised or malicious apps to inject fake payment screens into real storefronts. The customer sees the real brand; the card data goes somewhere else.

App sprawl after an agency handoff

A store changes agencies, keeps 14 apps, and nobody knows which ones still have write_orders or read_customers.

Scope

Six places checkout risk actually hides.

We review the public storefront first, then the admin-side app and theme configuration with your authorization. Everything is mapped to a finding ID, evidence, impact, and a fix.

Script & tag inventory

Every external domain, inline block, pixel, and tag. Which ones have an owner, which ones changed, which ones run on checkout.

Checkout path

Iframes, redirects, express-checkout buttons, and any element that asks for payment or customer data outside Shopify's checkout.

Headers & browser policy

CSP, HSTS, frame-ancestors, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy, and mixed content. The cheap fixes most themes never ship.

App permissions

Installed apps, OAuth scopes, last-used dates, unused apps, and apps that can read customers or write orders. Admin-assisted.

Theme code

Liquid changes, remote scripts, obfuscation, hard-coded keys, and drift from the upstream theme. We diff what is actually served.

Compliance surface

Privacy, terms, refund, shipping, cookie consent, and data-sharing language. Gaps that turn an incident into a legal problem.

FAQ

The questions a smart merchant asks.

Is this a penetration test?

No. The default review is passive: we fetch public pages, parse what the browser receives, and review configuration with your authorization. We do not exploit, brute-force, card-test, or attack third parties. If you need an active pentest, we will say so and scope it separately with written authorization.

Do you need admin access?

Not for the Storefront Review. For the Full Review, a limited collaborator account helps us see installed apps, OAuth scopes, last-used dates, and theme code. We ask for the minimum role that can read those areas, not the store owner account.

What if you find an active compromise?

We stop, document evidence, and give you an incident checklist: disable the app, rotate API keys and staff accounts, review recent orders and payment logs, preserve evidence, and contact Shopify Support. We do not contact your customers or processors on your behalf.

Do you fix the issues?

The report is written for your developer or agency. We can implement fixes as a separate, quoted work order if you want. We do not bundle unlimited remediation into a fixed-price review.

Is CartGuard affiliated with Shopify?

No. CartGuard is an independent review service. Shopify has its own security and app-review programs. Our value is the store-specific inventory, evidence, and prioritization that the platform does not provide for your exact configuration.

What do you need to start?

The store URL, the markets you sell in, a list of apps you know about, and a 15-minute call. If you have a recent agency handoff or a planned migration, tell us — that is exactly when script and app drift tends to appear.
